Theory · 01

What is the Present Simple?

It is the tense we use to talk about things that are generally true, regular or permanent — not just about this moment.

The Present Simple describes habits, facts, permanent situations, schedules and states.

iIt is one of the most common tenses in English — you use it every day to talk about yourself and the world.

Present Simple

I read books every evening.
→ a regular habit

Not right now

The action is not necessarily happening at the moment of speaking — it happens in general.

  • every day / every week / every summer
  • on Mondays / at weekends / in the morning
  • once a week / twice a month / three times a year
  • always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never
Theory · 02

Affirmative form

We use the base form of the verb. With he, she, it we add -s or -es.

I / You / We / They+verb
  • I work in an office.
  • We live in a big city.
  • They play tennis on Saturdays.
He / She / It+verb + s / es
  • He works in a hospital.
  • She watches TV in the evening.
  • It rains a lot in autumn.

Theory · 03

Spelling: -s, -es, -ies

The third person singular (he / she / it) follows a few simple spelling rules.

Most verbs just add -s.

work → works
read → reads
play → plays
like → likes

Verbs ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -o add -es.

watch → watches
wash → washes
fix → fixes
miss → misses
go → goes
do → does

Consonant + y → -ies. But vowel + y → just -s.

study → studies
fly → flies
try → tries
play → plays (vowel + y)

have

I have → she has

be

I am → he is → they are

Theory · 04

Negative form

We use the auxiliary do not (don't) or does not (doesn't) + the base form of the verb.

I / You / We / They+don't+verb
  • I don't like coffee.
  • They don't live here.
He / She / It+doesn't+verb
  • She doesn't eat meat.
  • He doesn't speak French.
!No -s on the main verb after doesn't: she doesn't eats → she doesn't eat.
  • We don't go to school on Sundays.
  • My brother doesn't play the guitar.
  • It doesn't snow in summer.
  • You don't need a ticket.
Theory · 05

Questions & short answers

Questions start with Do or Does. The main verb stays in the base form.

Do / Does+subject+verb…?
  • Do you like pizza?
  • Does she work here?
Wh-+do / does+subject+verb…?
  • Where do you live?
  • What time does the film start?
  • How often do they train?
QuestionYesNo
Do you like tea?Yes, I do.No, I don't.
Does he drive?Yes, he does.No, he doesn't.
Do they live here?Yes, they do.No, they don't.
Theory · 06

The verb to be

To be is special: it has its own forms and never uses do / does.

I

am — I am a student.

He / She / It

is — She is at home.

We / You / They

are — They are friends.

  • I am not (I'm not) tired.
  • He is not (isn't) busy.
  • We are not (aren't) late.
Am / Is / Are+subject+…?
  • Are you ready? — Yes, I am.
  • Is it cold today? — No, it isn't.
!Do you are ready? → Are you ready?

Habits & routines

Actions that we do regularly, again and again.

We use the Present Simple for things that happen repeatedly — daily routines, hobbies and habits.

  • I get up at 7 o'clock every day.routine
  • She drinks coffee in the morning.habit
  • We play football on Fridays.hobby
  • He brushes his teeth twice a day.routine

Use 2 of 5

Facts & general truths

Things that are always true — in science, nature and life in general.

Facts don't change, so we describe them with the Present Simple.

  • Water boils at 100 °C.
  • The Earth goes around the Sun.
  • Cats have four legs.
  • It snows in winter in Russia.

Science

Plants need light to grow. Ice melts at 0 °C.

Everyday truths

Shops close late on Fridays. Babies sleep a lot.

Use 3 of 5

Permanent situations

Situations that are true for a long time: where we live, work or study.

Use the Present Simple for long-term situations that are not likely to change soon.

  • My parents live in London.
  • He works for a big company.
  • She teaches English at a school.
  • We study at the same university.

Permanent → Present Simple

I live in Moscow.

Temporary → Present Continuous

I am living with my friend this month.

Use 4 of 5

Timetables & schedules

Official, fixed times — even for the future.

For transport, cinemas, lessons and events that follow a timetable, we use the Present Simple.

  • The train leaves at 7:15.
  • The film starts at 8 p.m.
  • Our lesson begins at 9 o'clock tomorrow.
  • The museum opens at 10 a.m.

Use 5 of 5

Likes, feelings & states

Stative verbs describe states, not actions. They are usually used in simple tenses.

We use the Present Simple to talk about what we like, think, know, want or have.

  • I love chocolate.
  • She knows the answer.
  • They want a new car.
  • I don't understand this word.

Feelings

like, love, hate, prefer

Mind

know, think, believe, understand, remember

Wants

want, need, wish

Possession

have, own, belong

Adverbs · 01

Adverbs of frequency

They tell us how often something happens. They are very common with the Present Simple.

An adverb of frequency answers the question How often…?

alwaysusuallyoftensometimesoccasionallyrarely / seldomhardly evernever
  • How often do you go to the gym? — I usually go three times a week.
  • How often does she cook? — She rarely cooks.
  • How often are they late? — They are never late.

One-word adverbs

always, usually, often, sometimes, never… → go in the middle of the sentence.

Expressions

every day, once a week, twice a month… → usually go at the end: I swim twice a week.

Word order · 01

Adverb + main verb

With ordinary (main) verbs, the adverb of frequency goes before the main verb.

Subject+adverb+main verb+…

She always gets up early.

subjectadverbmain verb
  • I usually walk to school.
  • He often plays computer games.
  • We sometimes visit our grandparents.
  • My mum never drinks coffee.
!He plays often games → He often plays games.
Subject+don't / doesn't+adverb+verb
  • I don't often eat fast food.
  • She doesn't usually work on Saturdays.
Word order · 02

To be + adverb

With the verb to be (am / is / are), the adverb goes after the verb.

Subject+am / is / are+adverb+…

Our teacher is often late.

subjectto beadverb
  • I am always hungry after school.
  • She is usually very kind.
  • They are sometimes tired.
  • It is never hot here in winter.
!She usually is kind → She is usually kind.
Subject+isn't / aren't / am not+adverb
  • He isn't usually late.
  • We aren't often at home on Fridays.
Word order · 03

Main verb vs. to be

Put the two rules side by side — the difference is easy to remember.

Main verbs → BEFORE

He often goes to the park.

They never eat fish.

To be → AFTER

He is often in the park.

They are never hungry.

“BE goes first, the adverb follows.
Other verbs wait — the adverb goes first.”

iThink of to be as the leader of the sentence: the adverb always walks behind it.

Usually, often, sometimes and occasionally can also go at the beginning or end of a sentence for emphasis:

  • Sometimes I walk to work.
  • I walk to work sometimes.
!Always, never, hardly ever, rarely and seldom normally stay in the middle: Always I get up early → I always get up early.
Questions · 01

Questions with main verbs

The adverb goes after the subject and before the main verb.

Do / Does+subject+adverb+main verb…?

Do you often go to the cinema?

  • Does she always walk to school?
  • Do they usually have lunch at home?
  • What do you usually do at weekends?
!Do often you play? → Do you often play?
  • In questions we use ever (= at any time): Do you ever eat sushi?
  • Answer: No, I never eat sushi. / Yes, I sometimes do.
  • How often does he go swimming?
Questions · 02

Questions with to be

Am / Is / Are comes first, then the subject, then the adverb.

Am / Is / Are+subject+adverb+…?

Is your teacher often late?

  • Are you always so busy?
  • Is he usually at home in the evening?
  • Are they ever late for class?
  • Why is she always so happy?
!Is often your teacher late? → Is your teacher often late?
QuestionYesNo
Are you usually tired?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Is she always on time?Yes, she is.No, she isn't.
Are they ever bored?Yes, sometimes.No, never.
